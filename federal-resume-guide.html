Federal résumé guide for USAJOBS applicants

Federal résumés should show how your actual experience meets the qualifications in the job announcement. Read the announcement carefully before you write: the eligibility, specialized experience, assessment questions, and required documents all matter.

How long should a federal résumé be?

USAJOBS now generally requires résumés of no more than two pages for federal job applications. Check the exact announcement and current USAJOBS résumé guidance before submitting, because exceptions and application instructions can vary.

What information should I include?

Use accurate employers, position titles, dates of employment, and hours worked per week when required. For prior federal positions, include series and grade where relevant. Give concrete examples of duties that match the vacancy's specialized experience. Education, licenses, certifications, and other claims must be truthful and supported by required documentation.

How does veterans' preference work?

Veterans' preference is not automatic for every former service member or hiring action. Eligibility and documentation depend on the applicant and the appointment method. Review the OPM Veterans' Preference Guide and the vacancy announcement. Direct-hire and other authorities can follow different selection rules.

Does military spouse preference apply?

Military spouse noncompetitive appointment authorities and spouse preference programs have separate eligibility rules. They do not guarantee an offer or apply to every federal vacancy. Review OPM's military spouse hiring guidance and the agency's instructions.

How should I tailor my federal résumé?

Identify the announcement's specialized experience and qualifications, then connect them to your real duties and accomplishments. Don't copy the vacancy text as if it were your own experience. Verify every date, grade, hour, credential, and achievement before submitting.
