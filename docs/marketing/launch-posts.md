## 2. LinkedIn: evidence before adjectives

“Helped with reports and training” can hide useful experience.

A better starting point is asking what you prepared, which tools you used, who used your work, and what you personally contributed. Numbers help only when they are real. Assisting a coordinator should never become “led the department.”

## 4. Military-transition guide

A civilian employer may not recognize a military acronym, but that doesn’t mean you need to invent a new job title.

Explain the task, the tools you actually used, and your own contribution. Keep assisting, training and supervising distinct. Don’t turn service experience into a credential you don’t hold.

I own CareerLaunch AI and published this free guide with a fictional example:

## 5. Employment-gap guide

An employment gap does not need a fabricated consulting job or an overly personal explanation.

Keep your dates accurate. Include coursework, projects or volunteering only when they actually happened. Focus on the relevant work you can describe truthfully now.
